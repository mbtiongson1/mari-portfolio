# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

**Primary: Peer technical writers and documentation leads.** They arrive to evaluate Mari's thinking process, learn from her approach to information architecture, and assess collaboration fit. They read closely, inspect structure, and judge the portfolio itself as a writing sample.

**Secondary: Hiring managers and recruiters** at mid-to-large enterprises (healthcare, tech, government). They scan for evidence of craft within 30 seconds. They need to see competence signals quickly, then depth if they stay.

**Also: Freelance clients and community organizations.** They need to understand what Mari does and whether her approach fits their documentation needs.

All visitors arrive via LinkedIn, job applications, or direct links to specific showcases. They are scanning first, reading second.

## Product Purpose

A portfolio for Maricar S. Tiongson ("Mari"), a technical writer with 5+ years in documentation and 11 years in community development. The portfolio showcases her documentation work through process-heavy case studies that reveal how she thinks about information architecture, not just what she produces.

The core content is **Showcases**: individual pieces of documentation work presented in three layers (artifact, process, impact), weighted heavily toward process. The portfolio teaches by showing. It is an act of generosity toward the technical writing community, not a sales pitch.

The timeline of Mari's career (Optum, Accenture, GECO Asia, Department of Agriculture, SSS, Pasko Ng Mga Bata) lives in an About section as context for the arc from community development to technical writing. It is not the main content.

Success: a peer technical writer visits, reads a showcase, and leaves having learned something about documentation craft. A hiring manager visits and within 30 seconds knows Mari is serious about her work.

## Brand Personality

**Grounded, generous, warm.**

- **Grounded**: rooted in real-world community work. The work has weight and consequence. Not Silicon Valley disruption energy.
- **Generous**: the portfolio is an act of sharing, not gatekeeping. Process sections feel like mentoring. "Here's how I thought through this so you can do it too."
- **Warm**: human, approachable, the specific warmth of someone who genuinely cares about the people her documentation serves. Not clinical precision; human precision.

The tension: warmth without sacrificing authority. Generosity without losing professional gravity. The design itself is evidence of the craft: a technical writer who builds a well-structured, beautiful portfolio is practicing what she preaches.

## Anti-references

1. **Dribbble portfolio templates**: giant "Hi, I'm [Name]" hero, floating browser mockups, identical project card grids, "Let's work together" CTA. Signals nothing about the person.
2. **Corporate case study sites**: stock photography, blue-and-white, "Challenge / Solution / Results" with bullet points and percentages. Consulting-deck energy.
3. **Minimalist dev portfolios**: dark mode, monospace, terminal aesthetic, "I build things" one-liner. Wrong register entirely.
4. **Content-creator personal brands**: gradient backgrounds, emoji in headings, "storyteller" in bio, testimonial carousels, newsletter popups. Marketing slop.

## Design Principles

1. **Practice what you preach.** The portfolio's information architecture, labeling, and structure are the first writing sample. Every structural choice should demonstrate the same care Mari brings to client documentation.
2. **Teach by showing.** Process sections are the heart. They don't explain what Mari did; they show how she thinks. The visitor should leave knowing something they didn't before.
3. **Earn every element.** No decorative noise. Every visual choice, every word, every section exists because it serves the reader. Restraint is respect for the visitor's time.
4. **Warmth is authority.** Approachability and professional gravity are not opposites. The design should feel like a conversation with someone who is both kind and deeply competent.
5. **The portfolio is the specimen.** The site itself, its readability, its navigation, its hierarchy, is evidence of documentation craft. It should withstand the scrutiny of peer technical writers inspecting the source.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

- WCAG 2.1 AA baseline (non-negotiable; a technical writer's portfolio failing contrast checks is a credibility failure)
- Semantic HTML landmarks: proper `<main>`, `<nav>`, `<article>` per showcase
- Visible, designed focus indicators (not hidden, not browser defaults)
- Skip-to-content links on showcase pages
- 18px+ body text, 1.5-1.6 line-height for body copy
- Keyboard-navigable showcase navigation with proper focus management
- `prefers-reduced-motion` respected
- `prefers-color-scheme` hooks in place
- Descriptive link text always (never "click here" or "read more")
